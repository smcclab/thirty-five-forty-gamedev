The theory programme is the conceptual half of the course. It gives you the
vocabulary and the models --- formal elements, engagement, systems, mechanics,
balance, interface, playtesting --- that you then apply in the
[workshops](/workshops/) and argue from in your
[assessments](/assessments/).

In 2024 the theory was delivered as short per-topic decks with embedded video,
worked through in your own time at roughly an hour a week, rather than as a
timetabled lecture. The one timetabled lecture was the week 1 orientation. The
decks below are converted from those 2024 slides.

## Topics week by week

Theory runs in weeks 1--5 and 7--9. Week 6 has no new theory --- that week is
for finishing your prototype and proposal --- and weeks 10--12 are given over
to the team project.

| Week | Decks | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Play and Games](/lectures/week01-1-play-and-games/); [Formal Elements](/lectures/week01-2-formal-elements/) | What play and games are, and the formal elements vocabulary the proposal asks you to write in |
| 2 | [Playcentric Design Process](/lectures/week02-1-playcentric-design-process/); [Idea Generation](/lectures/week02-2-idea-generation/); [Prototyping](/lectures/week02-3-prototyping/) | The iterative design method the whole course runs on, and how to generate and test ideas cheaply |
| 3 | [Engaging the player](/lectures/week03-1-engaging-the-player/); [Designing to engage](/lectures/week03-2-designing-to-engage/); [Flow, needs and motivation](/lectures/week03-3-flow-needs-motivation/) | Why players keep playing: challenge, choice, pleasure, flow, intrinsic motivation |
| 4 | [Systems](/lectures/week04-1-systems/); [Mechanics](/lectures/week04-2-mechanics/); [Balance](/lectures/week04-3-balance/) | Games as systems of objects, properties and behaviours; core mechanics; balancing them |
| 5 | [Interface Design](/lectures/week05-1-interface-design/); [Level Design](/lectures/week05-2-level-design/) | Camera, controls and display; shaping space and pacing through levels |
| 6 | No new theory | Prototype and proposal work |
| 7 | [Agile game development](/lectures/week07-1-agile-game-dev/) | Working as a team: iterations, backlogs, planning for the group project |
| 8 | [Learning and Training](/lectures/week08-1-learning-and-training/); [Challenge, difficulty and pacing](/lectures/week08-2-challenge-difficulty-pacing/) | Teaching the player to play; tuning difficulty curves and pacing |
| 9 | [The playtesting process](/lectures/week09-1-playtesting-process/); [Conducting playtesting](/lectures/week09-2-conducting-playtesting/) | Running a playtest that gives you usable data rather than compliments |

## How the theory connects to the rest of the course

Each block of theory is applied in that week's workshop and then cited in an
assessment:

- Weeks 1--5 feed the [Game Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/)
  directly. The proposal template asks for sections on formal elements
  (week 1), core gameplay and the prototyping process (week 2), engagement
  (week 3), mechanics (week 4) and interface (week 5), each with specific
  reference to the theory.
- Week 2 also feeds the [Prototype Progress](/assessments/progress/) report,
  which asks what changed through your prototyping process and what you learned
  from it.
- Week 7 supports team formation and planning for the
  [Game Project](/assessments/project/).
- Weeks 8--9 support the playtesting you run on the project, and the peer
  playtesting that marks it.

## Set texts

The decks cite two books by page throughout, so it is worth having access to
them:

- Tracy Fullerton. 2019. *Game Design Workshop: A Playcentric Approach to
  Creating Innovative Games*, 4th edition. CRC Press.
- Jesse Schell. 2020. *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*, 3rd edition.
  CRC Press.

The [reading list](/resources/reading/) maps chapters and page ranges to each
topic and lists the other works the decks draw on.
