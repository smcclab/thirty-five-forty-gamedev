The theory in this course is delivered as recorded [lectures](/lectures/) you
work through in your own time. The workshop is where you apply it. Each week
runs a short recap of the relevant theory followed by three to seven timed
activities, and nearly every activity produces something you keep: a
brainstorm, a diagram, a paper prototype, a written description, a plan, a set
of playtest notes.

Workshops run in weeks 2 to 12.

## The two halves

**Weeks 2 to 6 build your individual prototype.** You brainstorm ideas and
narrow them to one, set a player experience goal, work out the formal elements
and the core gameplay, prototype the game on paper, design its viewpoint,
controls and interface, and finally spend a whole workshop writing it up. This
work feeds the [Game Prototype Progress](/assessments/progress/) and [Game
Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/) submissions, and the proposal
is what makes you eligible to join a project team.

**Weeks 7 to 12 build the group game.** Teams of four to five form over the
teaching break. From week 7 the workshop is your team's meeting: you choose a
concept, plan sprints, run a scrum every week, work through training and
balancing for your game, script a playtest, run it with another team, and then
rehearse the peer assessment in the final week. This work feeds the [Game
Project](/assessments/project/) and your [Developer
Diary](/assessments/diary/).

| Weeks | Focus | Working as |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | Idea generation, pitching, formal elements | Individual, in table groups |
| 4–5 | Core gameplay, systems, physical prototyping, interface design | Individual, in pairs |
| 6 | Writing the proposal and design analysis | Individual |
| 7–8 | Team formation, project planning, agile process | Project team |
| 9 | Training the player, balancing | Project team |
| 10–12 | Playtesting: planning, running, peer marking | Project team |

## What to bring

- **Weeks 2 to 6:** paper, pens, and anything that works as a game token. Much
  of the early work happens away from a screen. From week 4 you also want your
  Unity project, and in week 6 your part-filled proposal template.
- **Weeks 7 to 12:** a laptop, your team's current build, and — from week 11 —
  a device you can playtest on plus a printed copy of your playtest script.

## What you should do each week

Every page has three sections. **Before the workshop** lists the preparation:
usually the theory to review and the Unity Junior Programmer Pathway unit to
finish. **Activities** gives the instructions in enough detail to follow along
or catch up. **After the workshop** is what to do before the next one.

:::tip
Photograph or save the output of every activity. Both the proposal and the
developer diary are marked partly on documented evidence of your process, and
only evidence included in the submitted document is considered — you cannot
link to it later.
:::
