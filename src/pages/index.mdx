## What is Game Development?

COMP3540/6540 is a studio course in making digital games. You learn what makes
play work --- the formal elements of games, the shape of player experience ---
and then put that to work by generating ideas, building prototypes, playtesting
them with real players, and producing a finished game in a team.

The course is built around the *playcentric* design process: you design for the
experience the player has, and you find out what that experience actually is by
putting the game in front of people early and often.

The focus is on functional, interactive design: systems, mechanics, interfaces
and iterative prototyping. Story and visual aesthetics are not covered.

## How the course runs

Three strands run in parallel:

| Strand | What it is | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Theory](/lectures/) | Short per-topic slide decks with videos, about an hour a week, taken in your own time | Weeks 1--5 and 7--9 |
| [Unity Learn](/resources/unity-pathway/) | The self-paced Unity Junior Programmer Pathway, roughly one unit a week | Weeks 1--6 |
| [Workshops](/workshops/) | Two timetabled hours a week of design activities, prototyping, team work and playtesting | Weeks 2--12 |

The theory gives you the vocabulary and the models. Unity Learn gives you the
engine skills. The workshops are where you actually design your game, and where
you get the evidence that the [assessments](/assessments/) ask you to reflect
on.

## What you make

You build an individual prototype in Unity in the first half of semester, using
primitives only so that the marks go to gameplay rather than to art. You write
a proposal analysing that prototype against the design theory. Then you form a
team, build a complete single-player game, publish it to Unity Play, and it is
played and marked by your peers. Alongside that you keep a developer diary of
your own contributions and learning.

See [assessments](/assessments/) for the full set, and
[past student games](/resources/showcase/) for what the course has produced
before.

Two course codes are available:
[COMP3540](https://programsandcourses.anu.edu.au/course/COMP3540) (undergraduate)
and [COMP6540](https://programsandcourses.anu.edu.au/course/COMP6540)

additional learning outcome on evaluating game development outcomes against
current industry practice.

If you have any issues with enrolment, follow the instructions on

permission code.

## Getting started

1. [Enrol in the course](https://cecc.anu.edu.au/students/continuing/enrolling/) and sign up for a [workshop](/workshops/) on MyTimetable.
2. Check you have a [laptop](/policies/#laptop) that can run Unity and that you can bring to every workshop.
3. Read the [tools](/resources/tools/) page, create a free Unity account and install the editor.
4. Start the [Unity Junior Programmer Pathway](/resources/unity-pathway/) in week 1 --- it is self-paced and the prototype assessments assume you are keeping up.
5. Get hold of the [set texts](/resources/reading/); the theory decks cite them by page throughout.
6. Read the [FAQ](/resources/faq/) if you are wondering whether this course is for you.

The course is assessed on process as much as product. Engage every week from
week 1, and collect evidence --- sketches, photos of paper prototypes,
screenshots, commit history --- as you go. Work that starts the week before a
due date does not pass.
