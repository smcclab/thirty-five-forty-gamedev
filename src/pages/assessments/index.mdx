Assessment in this course is one long build. You develop a game concept on
your own, prototype it in Unity, propose it in writing, and then make a
different game in a team -- keeping an individual record of your own work as
you go.

:::warning
The table below is the **2024** assessment structure, carried over as the
starting point while this site is rebuilt. Weights and dates are not settled
for the next offering until the class summary is published, about two weeks
before semester starts. Timing is given by teaching week throughout.
:::

## How the items chain together

The first half of the semester is individual. In weeks 1 to 4 you develop a
game concept in the [workshops](/workshops/) and build an early Unity
prototype of it, submitting both the prototype and a report on your
prototyping process as the
[Game Prototype Progress](/assessments/progress/) item. That item exists
mainly to get you feedback before census date, while there is time to change
course.

You then carry the same prototype forward. The
[Game Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/) is a more developed
build of it plus a written analysis of your design against the theory covered
in weeks 1 to 5, and a continuation of the same prototyping-process log for
weeks 5, 6 and the break. Submitting it is what qualifies you to be assigned
to a team.

From week 7 the course becomes a group project. Teams are formed after the
proposal deadline, meet in the [week 7 workshop](/workshops/week-07/), and
build a game together for the
[Game Project](/assessments/project/), which is marked by peer playtesting on
Unity Play. Because the game is marked as a team, the
[Developer Diary](/assessments/diary/) runs alongside it as the individual
record: two entries, covering weeks 7--9 and 10--12, logging the work you
personally did and what you learned doing it.

The [Research Participation and Reflection](/assessments/research-reflection/)
item runs independently across the whole semester and is marked satisfactory
or unsatisfactory. Start it early -- experiment availability is not grounds
for an extension.

Read the course [policies](/policies/) for submission, extensions, special
consideration and appeals.
