## Purpose

The Game Prototype Progress item asks you to show your progress towards the
[Game Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/) and to get early
feedback on your work, while there is still time to act on it. It falls before
census date deliberately: the feedback is meant to tell you whether you are on
track.

## Restrictions

Your prototype must meet all of these:

- **Single-player only.** Design the game to be played by one player.
- **Computer game.** Design it to be played on a computer (PC or Mac) with a
  keyboard and mouse.
- **Unity Play.** The prototype must be published as a WebGL build to Unity
  Play.
- **Primitives only.** No assets are permitted, including free, paid and
  self-made assets. Materials may be used; textures may not. Focus on
  gameplay.

:::warning
A 50% penalty applies to the prototype mark if assets or textures are used
(in 2024, -20 of the 40 prototype marks).
:::

## Prototype (4%)

Publish your prototype to Unity Play as a WebGL build. Name it
`ANU2024 <UID>`, substituting your own UID and the current year. Unity's own
guide to
[creating and publishing WebGL builds](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-and-publishing-webgl-builds)
covers the process.

:::warning
Any change to your Unity Play game after the due date counts as a late
submission and attracts late penalties. Stop editing at the deadline.
:::

### How the prototype is developed

The prototype comes out of two strands of work running side by side: the Unity
Junior Programmer Pathway, and the weekly [workshops](/workshops/).

In weeks 1 to 4 you are expected to complete Units 1 to 4 of the Unity Junior
Programmer Pathway. The labs in those units map onto what the prototype needs:

| Pathway lab | What it gives your prototype |
| --- | --- |
| Lab 2 -- New Project with Primitives | Background plane, primitive player, camera chosen for your game type, primitive enemies, obstacles, projectiles and materials |
| Lab 3 -- Player Control | A player controller with basic movement from player input, with appropriate constraints |
| Lab 4 -- Basic Gameplay | Basic gameplay: object movement, destruction, collisions and spawning |

The workshops develop the game concept the prototype is a digital
representation of:

| Workshop | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| [Week 2](/workshops/week-02/) | Player experience goals |
| [Week 3](/workshops/week-03/) | Game idea, formal elements |
| [Week 4](/workshops/week-04/) | Core gameplay and system, game foundation and structure |

Your prototype should clearly be a digital representation of the unique game
concept you have been developing through the workshop activities -- not a
generic pathway exercise.

## Progress report (8%)

Submit the Prototype Progress Report as a single Word document via Wattle,
using the provided template. Do not modify the template structure.

All word limits are maximums. Write only what you need, include the total word
count where the template asks for it, and expect your marker to stop reading
at the limit.

### What the template asks for

The header table records your UID and name, your game's name on Unity Play
(`ANU2024 <UID>`), the link to the game, your preferred browser for testing,
any instructions the marker needs in order to play the prototype, and a
description of the game concept in **100 words maximum** so the marker has
context.

The body has two parts.

**Prototyping Process (800 words maximum).** A table with one row per week for
weeks 1, 2, 3 and 4, and columns for Activities and Reflections. Describe your
workshop, Unity and personal prototyping activities and the reflections that
went with them. Keep the activity descriptions brief and spend the words on
the reflection: what changed through your prototyping process, and what you
discovered or learned. Do not change the time periods -- they must stay weeks
1 to 4.

**Prototyping Evidence.** A numbered table of images of your physical and
digital prototypes. Every activity in the process table must reference a
numbered piece of evidence here. Do not link to evidence held elsewhere; only
evidence included in the document is looked at.

## Marking

The prototype and the report are marked separately. In 2024 the prototype was
marked out of 40 and the report out of 80, for a grand total of 120 marks
scaled to the 12% weighting.

### Prototype rubric (4%)

The prototype rubric is a checklist: each criterion is judged satisfactory,
partial or unsatisfactory.

| Criterion | Weight | Marks | Satisfactory | Partial | Unsatisfactory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unity Play | 0.5% | 5 | Published to Unity Play at the URL given, named `ANU2024 <UID>`, and loads (5) | Published at the URL given and loads, but incorrectly named (3) | Missing or does not load (0) |
| Scene, player and objects | 0.5% | 5 | Game scene with player and key objects as primitives; different types of object look different through shape and/or material (5--4) | Game scene with player and some objects as primitives; objects may look similar (3--2) | Scene, player or objects missing (1--0) |
| Camera | 0.5% | 5 | Camera position and angle set, working, and appropriate to the game type (5--4) | Camera works but may not be appropriate, or has some issues (3--2) | Camera broken or has serious issues (1--0) |
| Controls | 0.5% | 5 | Player moves on user input with appropriate restrictions in place (5--4) | Player moves on user input, with some issues (3--2) | Player cannot move, or serious or numerous issues (1--0) |
| Gameplay | 1% | 10 | Basic gameplay in place -- object movement, destruction, collisions, spawning -- and the game is functional (10--7) | Some gameplay in place; some issues or missing basic functionality (6--4) | Limited or no gameplay, or serious issues (3--0) |
| Prototype | 0.5% | 5 | Clearly a prototype for the game concept described in the report, and maps to the prototyping work documented there (5--4) | Some apparent connection to the proposed concept (3--2) | Little or no connection to the game concept (1--0) |
| Playable | 0.5% | 5 | The prototype is playable and the core gameplay is evident (5--4) | Partially playable; core gameplay may not be clear (3--2) | Not playable (1--0) |
| Asset penalty | -- | -20 | No assets or textures used; primitives and materials only | -- | Assets or textures used: -20 marks |

### Progress report rubric (8%)

The report is marked week by week, out of 10 for each of weeks 1 to 4 on each
of two criteria.

| Criterion | Weight | Marks | What the marker looks for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prototyping Process Activities | 4% | 40 (10 per week) | Whether the prototyping activities carried out in workshops, in Unity and on your own are described clearly and concisely, each supported by a numbered piece of evidence that meaningfully backs it up, and whether they show an appropriate amount of effort in prototyping. Time periods must not be changed; links are not followed; only evidence in the document is considered. |
| Prototyping Process Reflections | 4% | 40 (10 per week) | Whether the reflections on those activities -- what changed through prototyping, what you learned -- are deep and insightful, and reflect genuine learning and iteration. |

Each week's 10 marks are awarded in these bands, on both criteria:

| Standard | Marks | Activities | Reflections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exceptional | 10--8 | Exceptional description, evidence and effort | Exceptional reflections on changes and learnings |
| Superior | 7 | Superior description, evidence and effort | Superior reflections on changes and learnings |
| Good | 6 | Good description, evidence and effort | Good reflections on changes and learnings |
| Satisfactory | 5--4 | Satisfactory description, evidence and effort | Satisfactory reflections on changes and learnings |
| Incomplete | 3--0 | Insufficient description, evidence or effort | Insufficient reflections on changes or learnings |

### Standards and moderation

Points the 2024 marking guide made, which are worth knowing whether you are
writing the report or marking it:

- Completing the work to a satisfactory standard is a pass, not a high
  distinction. Markers were told to expect most students around the credit
  range, with roughly 20% in the HD range.
- The report should cover *your* prototyping activities, not just the course
  activities you attended, and should show both design and physical (in-class)
  prototyping and digital (Unity) prototyping.
- Less work in week 1 is expected and marked leniently.
- On the asset policy the intent is that no art assets are used: basic shapes
  and colours. Work that pushes the limit, such as building models out of
  primitives, drew a warning rather than a penalty.
- Marking was budgeted at about 20 minutes per student, with feedback aimed at
  what to improve for the Proposal rather than at justifying the mark. In 2024
  marks had to be released before census date, which set a tight marking
  timeline.

## Where this leads

The prototype you submit here becomes the prototype you develop further for
the [Game Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/), and the prototyping
process table is the same instrument you fill in again there for weeks 5, 6
and the teaching break. See the [assessments overview](/assessments/) for how
the items chain together.
