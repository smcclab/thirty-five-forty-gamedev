## Purpose

You build the [Game Project](/assessments/project/) in a small team, and the
game is marked as a team. The Developer Diary is the individual half of that:
a personal log of the work *you* did, what you learned, and how you personally
contributed to the team project.

Entry 1 is effectively a practice run for entry 2 -- the criteria are
identical, entry 2 simply carries more marks, and the feedback on entry 1 is
written so you can act on it.

## Instructions

You write two entries over the life of the project. Each entry should:

- log the hours you worked and the details of the tasks you completed
- demonstrate the work you completed, with evidence such as sketches and code
- identify and discuss the technical challenges or questions you encountered
- identify and discuss the strategies you used to solve those challenges or
  answer those questions, and reflect on what you learned

The work you describe must be your own personal work and contribution. Where
work involved other team members, identify it as such and describe what your
own contribution to it was.

:::warning
Use the provided template and do not modify it. Each entry is submitted as a
single Word document via Wattle.
:::

## What the template requires

The header records your UID, name, diary entry number and the date.

**Teamwork check.** Four questions about how the team is going: whether all
members have been contributing, whether contributions are roughly equal,
whether the team needs a moderation session with the convenor, and free-text
comments on any problems. These answers are **not** used for assessment --
they exist so that team problems surface early enough to be dealt with.

**Work log.** One row per task, with the task description (referencing the
numbered evidence), the dates worked and the number of hours. These are tasks
you personally completed, or your personal contribution to a group task.
Every task needs at least one piece of associated evidence. There is no word
limit, but be concise.

**Challenges or questions (200 words maximum).** Two key technical challenges
or questions you encountered in this period -- things you had to research or
work out how to solve. Personal, not group, challenges.

**Strategies and learnings (600 words maximum).** For each of the two
challenges, the strategies you used to solve it or answer it, and what you
learned in the process. Personal reflections, not group ones.

**Work evidence.** Numbered evidence of the work you completed: sketches,
code. Everything here must be numbered and referenced from the work log, and
every task needs at least one piece. Do not include links -- only evidence
included in the document is looked at. Images of change logs or repository
histories are unlikely to be accepted as meaningful evidence.

All word limits are maximums. Include the word count where the template asks
for it; your marker stops reading at the limit.

## Marking

Both entries are marked against the same three criteria, with entry 2 worth
more. In 2024 entry 1 was marked out of 80 and entry 2 out of 120.

| Criterion | Entry 1 | Entry 2 | What the marker looks for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Work log | 2% (20 marks) | 3% (30 marks) | Details of the individual tasks completed, with dates and hours for each, each supported by numbered evidence that meaningfully backs it up. Descriptions clear and concise; dates within the correct period (entry 1 weeks 7--9, entry 2 weeks 10--12). Whether the log shows an appropriate amount of individual effort. |
| Challenges or questions | 2% (20 marks) | 3% (30 marks) | Two key technical challenges or questions, described clearly and concisely, and complex enough to have required research and learning. |
| Strategies and learnings | 4% (40 marks) | 6% (60 marks) | Whether the discussion of the strategies used is clear, concise and meaningful, and whether the reflections on learning are deep, insightful and genuine. |

### Standards and expectations

From the 2024 marking guides:

- Total effort per entry was expected to be around 30 hours, including
  workshops (about 6 hours) and team meetings.
  their own: what you achieved and how well you described and evidenced it
  matters more.
- Diaries should focus on what the individual did and contributed, and on
  their own reflections and learning.
- "Technical" challenges are read broadly, covering design and development,
  but not team management or asset hunting.
- Completing the work to a satisfactory standard is a pass, not a high
  distinction.
- Written feedback was given on entry 1 only, aimed at what to improve for
  entry 2. Entry 2 was returned without comments because there is nothing left
  to act on.

:::info
The teamwork check is the mechanism for raising a problem in your team early.
If contributions are uneven, say so in entry 1 rather than waiting for the
coversheet at the end of the project.
:::
