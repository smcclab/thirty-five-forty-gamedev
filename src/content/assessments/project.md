## Purpose

The Game Project asks you to demonstrate your proficiency in Unity and your
ability to work in a team to apply game design concepts, and to complete and
publish a playable, enjoyable game.

## Restrictions

- **Single-player only.** The game must be playable by one player.
- **Computer game.** It must be playable on a computer (PC or Mac) with a
  keyboard and mouse.
- **Unity Play.** It must be published as a WebGL build to Unity Play.
- **15 minutes.** Peers and tutors will play your game for 15 minutes only.

The 15-minute rule is a design constraint, not just a marking convenience.
Markers play for 15 minutes whether your game is shorter or longer: a short
game gets played more than once, a long game gets cut off at 15 minutes.
Think about what kind of game experience actually works within that time, on
that device, on that platform, and aim to deliver something enjoyable,
complete and functional inside it.

## Game (35%)

Publish your finished, playable game to Unity Play as a WebGL build. Unity
have a [video walkthrough of publishing a WebGL build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c8BZJQvEq4g).

:::warning
Any change to your Unity Play game after the due date counts as a late
submission and attracts late penalties.
:::

### Asset policy

Unlike the individual prototypes, the group project may use assets, within
limits:

- **Permitted:** free assets from the Unity Asset Store, the Unity Course
  Library, and approved websites and repositories. The list of approved sites
  is maintained on the Unity Help forum on Wattle.
- **Graphics, sound and animation only.** Assets for code or functionality are
  not permitted.
- **No paid assets**, even if you did not pay for them -- gifts, giveaways and
  sale items all count as paid. If you want to make a case for a very cheap
  paid asset, email the convenor with the request and your justification
  before using it.
- **Everything must be declared** in the assets table on the coversheet, with
  its source and licence.

## Coversheet (0%, mandatory)

Submit the coversheet to Wattle as a PDF, using the template. **Every member
of the team submits it individually**, because your mark and feedback come
back through the coversheet item in Wattle.

The coversheet records:

- the game's name on Unity Play, its link, and your preferred browser for
  testing
- the **assets log**: one row per asset, with its source and licence
- **team details and contribution**: every member's UID, name, percentage
  contribution and signature

The default contribution is an equal split -- 25% each for a four-person team.
If the table records an unequal split, individual marks may be adjusted up or
down accordingly. By signing, each member confirms that the game is the team's
own work apart from the declared assets, that the game has been published to
Unity Play under the stated name, that only the listed assets were used, and
that they understand a mark of 0 follows if peer assessments are not completed
on time.

## Peer assessment (0%, mandatory)

After the game is submitted, you are assigned two to three other teams' games.
You play each one on Unity Play for 15 minutes on a computer with a keyboard
and mouse, and fill in an MS Form with your assessment.

Peer assessment must be completed in full, to a satisfactory quality, and on
time. If it is not, the individual mark for the Game is 0.

## Marking

Your game is marked by your peers through playtesting, using an MS Form.
Each statement below is rated on a five-point scale: strongly disagree,
disagree, neutral, agree, strongly agree.

| Area | Statements rated by playtesters |
| --- | --- |
| Functionality | The game was playable |
| Goals and learning | The goals of the game were clear; I understood how to play; I knew how to win or complete the game; it was easy to learn how to play; it was fun to learn how to play |
| Controls and interface | The controls were easy to use; the interface was clear; I knew my status or score; I was given feedback on my actions |
| Usability | I did not feel confused; I did not feel frustrated |
| Interest and challenge | I wanted to win or complete the game; the game offered interesting choices; the level of challenge was right for me; the gameplay was interesting |
| Pacing | The pacing was right for me |
| Originality | The game was original or innovative |
| Enjoyment | I enjoyed playing the game; I wanted to keep playing the game |

Markers are also asked to write feedback for the team: positive feedback on
what the team did well, and constructive criticism of what could be improved.
That feedback is passed back to you.
