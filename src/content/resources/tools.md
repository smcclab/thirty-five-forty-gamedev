## Unity

The course is taught in [Unity](https://unity.com/). You do not need previous
Unity experience; the [Unity pathway](/resources/unity-pathway/) takes you from
nothing to a working game over the first half of semester.

What you need:

1. **A free Unity account.** Create one at
   [id.unity.com](https://id.unity.com/). The same account gets you Unity Learn
   and Unity Play.
2. **The Unity Hub and the Unity editor.** Download from
   [unity.com/download](https://unity.com/download). Install the editor version
   the course specifies (**TODO**) and, importantly, add the **WebGL Build
   Support** module --- everything you submit is a WebGL build, and adding the
   module afterwards means a second long download.
3. **A code editor.** Unity installs Visual Studio or Visual Studio Code
   alongside the editor; either is fine. You write C#.

:::tip
Install and build a WebGL player once in week 1, before you have anything worth
losing. WebGL builds are slow and the first one always surfaces a missing
module or a disk space problem.
:::

### Hardware

You need a laptop that runs the Unity editor and that you can bring to every
workshop. Unity is more demanding than most coursework software: a discrete GPU
is not required, but a machine that struggles with the editor will cost you
hours every week.

## Unity Play

Prototypes and the final game are published as WebGL builds to
[Unity Play](https://play.unity.com/), which is also how they are submitted,
played and marked. Peer markers play your published build in a browser --- if
it does not load there, it cannot be marked.

Unity's own guide,
[Creating and publishing WebGL builds](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-and-publishing-webgl-builds),
covers the process.

In 2024 builds had to be named to a set convention that included the year and
your university ID, and any change to a published build after the deadline
counted as a late submission. Check the current naming convention in the
[assessment specifications](/assessments/).

## Prototyping materials

A good deal of the design work happens off the screen. Paper prototypes, index
cards, dice, counters and sketches are used throughout the early
[workshops](/workshops/), and photographs of them are the evidence your
[progress report](/assessments/progress/) and [proposal](/assessments/proposal/)
are built on. Bring something to draw with and something to photograph with.

## Assets

The individual prototypes are **primitives only** --- cubes, spheres, capsules
and materials, no textures and no imported assets of any kind. The group
project allows free assets for graphics, sound and animation, from the Unity
Asset Store, the Unity Course Library and an approved list, with every asset
declared. The full rules and the penalties are on the
[policies](/policies/#assets) page.
