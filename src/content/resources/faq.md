## Skills and background

### Do I need to have made a game before?

No. The orientation lecture asks the class whether they have developed a game
before and whether they have ever used a games engine, and the course is
designed on the assumption that many people answer no to both. The
[Unity pathway](/resources/unity-pathway/) starts from nothing.

### Do I need to be a strong programmer?

You need to be comfortable writing code and reading errors. The Unity pathway
teaches the C# you need for the prototypes, and it teaches it by having you
build and then debug working projects. If you have done any prior programming
course, the engine is the new thing, not the language.

That said, this is a course about design, and the marks reward design. A
technically modest game that is clear, learnable and enjoyable scores better
than an ambitious one that is confusing.

### I cannot draw. Is that a problem?

No, and for half the semester it is explicitly not allowed to matter. The
individual prototypes are **primitives only** --- cubes, spheres and capsules,
with materials but no textures --- precisely so that the marks go to gameplay
rather than to art. For the group project you may use free assets from the
Unity Asset Store and approved repositories for graphics, sound and animation,
so nobody has to make art from scratch. See the
[asset policy](/policies/#assets).

### Do I need to be a gamer?

It helps to have played games attentively, because much of the course asks you
to analyse why a game works. It is not a requirement, and "attentively" matters
more than "a lot".

## Tools and equipment

### What engine does the course use?

Unity, with C#. This is not negotiable: the prototypes and the final game must
be published as WebGL builds to Unity Play so that they can be peer-playtested
in a browser. See [tools](/resources/tools/).

### Can I use Godot, Unreal or my own engine?

No. The assessment depends on everyone's game being playable by their peers in
a browser through the same platform, and the Unity pathway is the taught
content.

### Do I need my own laptop?

Yes, and you need to bring it to every workshop. It has to run the Unity
editor. The minimum specification for the next offering is **TODO**; see
[the laptop requirement](/policies/#laptop).

### Does any of this cost money?

No. A personal Unity account, the editor and Unity Play are free, and paid
assets are not permitted. The set texts are the only expense, and you should
check library access before buying anything.

### Do I need version control?

Not in 2024 --- the course did not assess Git, and prototypes were submitted
through Unity Play. Using version control on your team project is still a very
good idea, and the developer diary is easier to write if you have a commit
history to look back at.

## How the course runs

### Is there a lecture I have to attend?

In 2024 the only timetabled lecture was the week 1 orientation. The course ran
as a flipped classroom: the [theory](/lectures/) was delivered as short decks
with pre-recorded video, worked through in your own time at about an hour a
week, and the timetabled contact was the two-hour weekly
[workshop](/workshops/) in weeks 2--12, run by the convener with tutor support.

### Do I have to attend workshops?

Attendance is not marked directly, but the workshops are where you do the
design activities that the prototype assessments ask you to document and
provide evidence for. Missing them costs you marks in the written components.

### How much work is it outside class?

The Unity pathway alone is about four to five hours a week in weeks 1--6, on
top of workshops, theory and your own prototyping. The second half of semester
is team project work. Treat it as a normal full-weight course that is
front-loaded with tutorial work.

### What if I fall behind on the Unity pathway?

Catch up immediately rather than skipping ahead. Each unit builds on the last,
and the prototype assessments assume the skills from units 1--5. Week 6 has no
new theory partly to give you room to catch up.

## Teams

### Do I choose my own team?

In 2024 you could choose your own team from within your own workshop group, or
be allocated to one if you did not. Team size was set from enrolment numbers,
typically four or five.

### When are teams formed?

Part way through semester, after the individual proposal is submitted. Teams
were formed over the mid-semester break in 2024, with team activities starting
in the week 7 workshop.

### Do I have to submit the proposal to be in a team?

Yes. In 2024, submitting the [Game Proposal and Prototype](/assessments/proposal/)
was a condition of being assigned to a group for the
[Game Project](/assessments/project/), and the same applied to the earlier
[Prototype Progress](/assessments/progress/) item.

### Does the team build the game I proposed?

Not necessarily. Every member of the team has written their own individual
proposal and prototype, and the team has to agree on what to build together.
Your proposal is marked on its own terms, as an individual assessment, well
before teams are formed.

### How is individual contribution accounted for?

Through the [developer diary](/assessments/diary/), which is a personal log of
what you did, when, and what you learned, with evidence. Work done jointly must
be identified as such.

## Assessment

### How is the final game marked?

By peer playtesting. Each game is played for exactly fifteen minutes by peers
and tutors on a PC or Mac with keyboard and mouse, through its Unity Play
build, and rated on twenty statements about playability, clarity, learning,
challenge, pacing, originality and enjoyment. The full survey is on the
[Game Project](/assessments/project/) page.

### What happens if I do not complete my peer marking?

In 2024 the coversheet and the peer assessment were both worth 0% but both were
compulsory: an individual mark of 0 was given for the game if either was
missing, late, or not done properly.

### Can my game be multiplayer, or use a controller, or take an hour to play?

No. Games must be single-player, playable on a PC or Mac with keyboard and
mouse, and designed around a fifteen-minute play session. See
[platform and format restrictions](/policies/#restrictions).

### What is the research participation component?

A small assessment item requiring participation in approved games or
human--computer interaction experiments, plus a short reflection. Book early:
availability is limited and extensions were not granted for it in 2024. See
[research participation](/assessments/research-reflection/).

## Getting help

### Where do I ask questions?

On the course forums, not by email. In 2024 there were separate forums for
Unity support, general and assessment questions, and research participation,
and emailed questions were redirected to them. The Unity support forum was
answered by an experienced game developer hired for the course, so technical
questions about your own project went to someone who could actually debug them.
Personal matters go to the convener; extension requests go through the
university's extension application.
