The first sprint is under way. This workshop runs the team routine you will
repeat for the rest of the course -- scrum, review, reflect -- and then gives
you class time to start the first [Developer Diary](/assessments/diary/)
entry.

## Before the workshop

- Work on your first sprint towards a playable version.
- Follow your communication plan to keep in touch with your team.
- Review the [Developer Diary](/assessments/diary/) requirements.
- Keep a log of your work.
- Start your first diary entry and bring it to class.

## Recap: agile game development

Agile is a philosophy, not a prescribed methodology, and most game developers
use it in some form. The core elements (Schell p.103--105) are flexible goals,
a prioritised backlog, sprints of a few weeks with a concrete working
deliverable, short scrum meetings, a demo day at the end of each sprint, and a
retrospective to reflect on the process.

## Activities

### Activity 1: Scrum (15 min)

Run a scrum meeting for your team. Each person reports three things:

1. what they did yesterday, or since the last meeting;
2. what they will do today, or before the next meeting;
3. what problems they are facing.

Solutions are discussed *after* the meeting, not during it. Keep it short, and
stand up.

### Activity 2: Team review (30 min)

Review the goals and priorities, schedule and sprints, and communication plan
you made in [Week 7](/workshops/week-07/). Do you need to adjust anything?

- Review your project goals and priorities -- have they changed?
- Review your overall schedule and sprints -- are you on track?
- Review your communication plan -- is it working?
- Review your team contributions -- is everyone contributing?

### Activity 3: Team reflection (15 min)

Reflect on the issues and opportunities raised in your scrum and team review.

- What is working? What are you doing well?
- What isn't working? What issues are you facing?
- What are your opportunities for improving how you work?
- What do you need to change? What are your next steps?

If your team is experiencing issues you cannot resolve yourselves, ask your
tutor about a moderation session.

### Activity 4: Developer Diary (30 min)

Your [Developer Diary](/assessments/diary/) is a personal log of the work
*you* have done, what you have learned, and how you have personally
contributed to the team project. There are two entries, covering weeks 7--9
and weeks 10--12.

Each entry logs the dates and hours you worked and the tasks you completed,
demonstrates the work (sketches, code), identifies the challenges or questions
you encountered, and discusses the strategies you used and what you learned.

Work individually on each part in turn, about ten minutes each.

**Work log.** List the individual tasks you completed, with the dates and
hours worked on each -- one task per row. These are tasks you personally
completed, or your personal contribution to a group task. Each task needs a
corresponding, numbered piece of evidence referenced from the evidence table.
Keep descriptions clear and concise, and make sure the dates fall in the
correct period for the entry. Links are not accepted; only evidence included
in the document is considered.

**Challenges or questions.** Identify and describe two key *technical*
challenges or questions you encountered this period -- things that required
you to research or seek answers. They should be your personal
challenges, not the group's, and complex enough to require real learning.

**Strategies and learnings.** Discuss the strategies you used to solve those
challenges or answer those questions, and what you learned. These are your
personal reflections, and this is where you demonstrate what you have
personally learned, so give it the most space.

:::tip
Write the diary as you go, not at the end. The work log needs real dates and
hours, and the evidence is much easier to collect while you are doing the
work.
:::
