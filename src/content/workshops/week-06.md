individual prototype. It is a working session on the [Game Proposal and

## Recap: what the assessment asks for

The Game Proposal and Prototype has two parts.

**The prototype** is developed by working through the Unity Junior Programmer
Pathway and participating in the workshops, and published as a WebGL game to
Unity Play. It is marked on its game interface (camera, controls and user
interface), its gameplay (movement, rules and behaviours), whether it is
clearly a prototype for the concept described in the proposal, whether it is
playable with clear instructions, and its originality.

**The analysis** is written using the template, and has two halves:

- a **game design analysis** of how the proposal uses game design theory --
  core gameplay, formal elements, engagement, mechanics and interface; and
- a **prototyping process analysis** of how you revised the design through
  prototyping, with evidence.

:::warning
Asset policy: primitives only, including materials but not textures. No assets
are permitted, as with [Game Prototype Progress](/assessments/progress/).
:::

## Activities

### Activity 1: Core gameplay (10 min)

Working individually. Describe the core gameplay -- the actions a player
repeats most often while striving to achieve the game's overall goal. Fill in
the Core Gameplay box in the template.

You wrote this in [Week 4](/workshops/week-04/). Find it, paste it in, and
polish it.

Useful shapes to copy:

- *WarCraft III*: players build and move units on a map in real time with the
  intent of engaging opposing units in combat and destroying them.
- *Diablo III*: players battle monsters, seek treasure and explore dungeons in
  an attempt to amass wealth and become more powerful.
- *Monopoly*: players buy and improve properties with the goal of charging
  rent to other players who land on them in the course of play.
- *Super Mario Bros.*: a player controls Mario, making him walk, run and jump,
  while avoiding traps, overcoming obstacles and gathering treasure.

### Activity 2: Formal elements (25 min)

Working individually. Describe the formal elements of your prototype and
explain why the key decisions were made. All prototypes are single-player, so
you do not need to include that in the table. Fill in the Formal Elements
table in the template.

| Element | Questions |
|---|---|
| Players | How many? Requirements? Roles? |
| Objectives | What is the objective of the game? |
| Procedures | What are the required actions for play? |
| Rules | Any limits on player actions? Rules for behaviour? |
| Resources | What objects are valuable to players? Why? |
| Conflict | What causes conflict in the game? |
| Boundaries | What are they? Physical or conceptual? |
| Outcome | What are the potential outcomes? |

You did this in [Week 3](/workshops/week-03/) -- find it, paste it in, and
finish it. The **explanation** carries the marks, so leave enough words for
it: a few in-depth explanations beat many brief ones. Focus on what is unique
or interesting about your game, and on the core gameplay.

### Activity 3: Engagement (10 min)

Working individually. How do you engage the player in your game -- through
play, challenge, choice, pleasure, and so on? Give specific examples with
reference to the theories discussed in the Week 3 theory material. Fill in the
Engagement box.

Provide references, to specific lecture slides, textbook pages or other
materials, and list them in the References table.

### Activity 4: Mechanics (10 min)

Working individually. What are the core mechanics in your game, and why? What
system dynamics and information structures are used? What will be the key
challenges in balancing your game, and why? Give specific examples with
reference to the Week 4 theory material. Fill in the Mechanics box, and add
your references.

### Activity 5: Game interface (10 min)

Working individually. What interface design choices did you make, and why? How
does the interface support your gameplay? Give specific examples with
reference to the Week 5 theory material, which you worked through in
[Week 5](/workshops/week-05/). Fill in the Interface box, and add your
references.

### Activity 6: Prototyping process (20 min)

Working individually. Describe your workshop, Unity and personal prototyping
activities, and your reflections on them. Keep the activity descriptions brief
and put the words into the reflections: what changed through your prototyping
process, and what did you discover or learn? Fill in the Prototyping Process
table.

**Prototyping evidence.** Provide images of your physical and digital
prototypes as supporting evidence. Number each image and reference it from the
table. Links are not accepted -- only evidence included in the document is
considered, and the evidence has to meaningfully support the activity it is
attached to.
