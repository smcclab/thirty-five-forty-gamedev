The final workshop. It simulates the peer assessment that determines the [Game
Project](/assessments/project/) mark: you play two assigned games for 15
minutes each, fill in the marking sheet, and then read the feedback written
about your own game and turn it into the goals for your last sprint.

:::warning
If your game is not published to Unity Play before this workshop, you will
still playtest other teams' games, but you will not receive any feedback on
your own.
:::

## Before the workshop

- Work on your sprint towards a playable version.
- Publish a playable version of your game to Unity Play so it can be tested in
  this workshop.
- Bring devices you can use to play games on Unity Play -- ideally one per team
  member.

## In the workshop

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Activity 1: Scrum |
| 15 min | Activity 2: Prepare for playtesting |
| 45 min | Activity 3: Playtest games on Unity Play |
| 30 min | Activity 4: Playtesting review |

## The marking sheet

Each statement is rated on a five-point scale from strongly disagree to
strongly agree. Markers also write positive feedback (what worked well, what
they liked most) and constructive criticism (what could be improved for the
final version).

| Theme | Statements |
|---|---|
| Playability and clarity | The game was playable; the goals of the game were clear; I understood how to play; I knew how to win or complete the game; the controls were easy to use; the interface was clear; I knew my status or score; I was given feedback on my actions |
| Learning | It was easy to learn how to play; it was fun to learn how to play; I did not feel confused; I did not feel frustrated |
| Engagement | I wanted to win or complete the game; the game offered interesting choices; the level of challenge was right for me; the pacing was right for me; the gameplay was interesting |
| Overall | The game was original or innovative; I enjoyed playing the game; I wanted to keep playing the game |

## Activities

### Activity 1: Scrum (10 min)

Run a scrum meeting for your team. Each person reports what they did since the
last meeting, what they will do before the next one, and what problems they
are facing.

### Activity 2: Prepare for playtesting (15 min)

In your team, get ready to play games on Unity Play.

- Is your game published and ready to be tested? Tutors collect the names of
  the published games.
- Do you have a device you can play on? You will use it to test another team's
  game today. If you don't have one, observe a team member playtesting.
- You will be assigned two games to playtest and give feedback on.

### Activity 3: Playtest games on Unity Play (45 min)

Working individually, or with observers depending on devices, play your two
assigned games and fill out the marking sheet.

1. Play the first assigned game for 15 minutes, then fill out the sheet.
2. Play the second for 15 minutes, then fill out the sheet.

Tutors collect the sheets and give them to the team that made the game.

Give both positive and constructive feedback, and write it to help the team
improve their final game. Consider the kind of feedback you would like to
receive.

### Activity 4: Playtesting review (30 min)

Return to your team and discuss the session.

- Read the feedback you received from other students. Does it match your
  expectations? Is there anything surprising? What are the key issues
  identified? Was there anything they really enjoyed?
- Did you learn or observe anything from playtesting other teams' games?
- What are the highest-priority things to fix or improve in the time you have
  left? Make these the goals of your final sprint.

## After the workshop

To receive marks for the [Game Project](/assessments/project/) you must:

- submit your game to Unity Play;
- submit your coversheet; and
- complete the peer assessment.

You also need to submit your final [Developer Diary](/assessments/diary/)
entry. Check the [assessments](/assessments/) pages for the current due dates.
