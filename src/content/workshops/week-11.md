The first real playtest. Your team pairs up with another team and you take
turns running the script you wrote in [Week 10](/workshops/week-10/) on each
other's builds, then regroup to work out what the findings mean for your next
sprint.

:::warning
You need a playable version published to Unity Play *before* this workshop,
devices to test on, and your script in a form you can read while the game is
running on the test machine.
:::

## Before the workshop

- Work on your sprint towards a playable version.
- Publish a playable version to Unity Play.
- Bring devices you can playtest on -- ideally one per team member.
- Finalise your playtesting script and bring it. Print it if you can;
  otherwise bring a second device to read it from.

## In the workshop

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 15 min | Activity 1: Scrum |
| 10 min | Activity 2: Prepare for playtesting |
| 60 min | Activity 3: Playtest with another team |
| 15 min | Activity 4: Playtesting review |

## Activities

### Activity 1: Scrum (15 min)

Run a scrum meeting for your team. Each person reports what they did since the
last meeting, what they will do before the next one, and what problems they
are facing.

### Activity 2: Prepare for playtesting (10 min)

In your team, get ready to playtest with another team.

- How many computers do you have to test on, and how many people can you test
  at once? Ideally every team member brings a device and runs a separate test
  -- you get far more feedback that way.
- If you don't have one device per member, who runs the tests? What are the
  other members doing: taking notes, observing?
- Start up your game and make sure it is working and ready to be played.

### Activity 3: Playtest with another team (60 min)

Working with another team, take turns to playtest each other's games using
your scripts.

- If you have enough devices, work in pairs with members of the other team, so
  every person gets to playtest the game.
- If you don't, form small groups so some members playtest or run the session
  while others observe and take notes.
- Follow your script, and take lots of notes.
- Keep track of time and stop playing after 15 minutes.
- Once you have completed the playtest in your pairs, switch roles and test
  the other team's game.

Remember your role from [Week 10](/workshops/week-10/): you are an observer,
not a designer. Don't answer the tester's questions -- ask what they think.
Don't defend the game. Write the criticisms down.

### Activity 4: Playtesting review (15 min)

Return to your team and discuss the sessions.

- Take turns to summarise your session. What did you learn? What were the key
  issues? Was there anything they really enjoyed? How are you tracking against
  your player experience goals?
- Compare your findings. Did different playtesters have similar experiences
  and find similar issues? What are the most important things you learned?
- Where to next? Do the findings change your goals, plans or priorities? What
  does this mean for your next sprint?

## After the workshop

- Work on your sprint towards a playable version.
- Publish a playable version of your game to Unity Play to be tested in the
  next workshop. [Week 12](/workshops/week-12/) is a practice run of the peer
  assessment, and you only get indicative feedback on the marking rubric if
  your beta is published.
- Bring devices you can use to play games on Unity Play -- ideally one per team
  member.
