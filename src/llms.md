# COMP3540/6540 --- Game Development

> Course website for COMP3540/COMP6540 Game Development at the ANU School of Computing: the theory decks, weekly workshops, assessments and resources for a studio course in designing, prototyping and building digital games in Unity.

COMP3540/6540 Game Development is a course at the ANU School of Computing,
Australian National University, in which students design, prototype and build
digital games. COMP3540 is the undergraduate code and COMP6540 the
postgraduate one; they are taught together, with one additional learning
outcome for COMP6540 on evaluating game development outcomes against current
industry practice.

The course is built around Tracy Fullerton's playcentric design process. It
covers play and games, the formal elements of games, idea generation,
prototyping, player engagement and motivation, systems, mechanics and balance,
interface and level design, agile game development, learning and difficulty,
and playtesting. Its focus is functional, interactive design; story and visual
aesthetics are not covered. The set texts are Fullerton's *Game Design
Workshop* (4th edition) and Jesse Schell's *The Art of Game Design* (3rd
edition).

Three strands run in parallel: self-paced theory decks (weeks 1--5 and 7--9),
Unity's self-paced Junior Programmer Pathway (weeks 1--6), and a timetabled
two-hour weekly workshop (weeks 2--12). Students build an individual Unity
prototype using primitives only, write a proposal analysing it against the
design theory, then form teams to build a complete single-player game published
as a WebGL build to Unity Play and marked by peer playtesting, while keeping an
individual developer diary. A small component requires participation in games
or HCI research.
