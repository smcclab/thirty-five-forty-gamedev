## The Structure of Games


### Formal elements form the structure of a game

- Games have Players (active participants)
- Games have Objectives that players try to achieve
- Players use Procedures to achieve objectives
- Players abide by Rules that govern the game
- Games have Resources that are valuable to the players
- Conflict arises from relationship between rules and objectives
- The game takes place within specified Boundaries
- The game drives towards an unequal Outcome

## Players


### Games are designed for players

- Number of players (single/multiple, set/variable)
- Roles of players (uniform/different, balance)

### Player interaction patterns

- Single player vs. game
- Multiple individual players vs. game
- Player vs. player (2 players)
- Unilateral competition (2+ vs. 1)
- Multilateral competition (3+ players)
- Cooperative play (2+ vs. game)
- Team competition (2+ groups)

## Objectives


### Define what players are trying to accomplish within the game rules

- Challenging but achievable
- Can set tone of the game
- Different objectives for different players
- Players choose from several objectives
- Partial objectives to help players achieve main objective
- For you to think about:
- What are some objectives of games you’ve played?
- What impact do these objectives have on the tone of the game?
- Do certain game types lend themselves to certain objectives?
- What about multiple objectives? Or player-defined objectives?
- Do objectives need to be explicit?

## Objectives


### Common objective categories

- Capture – take something of opponent’s (terrain or units) without being captured or killed (e.g., chess/checkers, capture the flag)
- Chase – catch or elude opponent
- Race – reach a goal (physical or conceptual) before other players
- Alignment – arrange game pieces (e.g., puzzles, Tetris, tic-tac-toe)
- Rescue or Escape – get defined units to safety (e.g., Super Mario Bros)
- Forbidden Act – get competition to “break the rules” (e.g., Twister)
- Construction – build or maintain or manage objects (e.g., Minecraft)
- Exploration – explore game areas (e.g., Zelda, GTA)
- Solution – solve a puzzle before the competition (e.g., Tetris, Zelda)
- Outwit – gain and use knowledge to defeat players (e.g., trivia)

## Procedures


### Methods of play and actions players can take to achieve the objectives


### Who does what, when, and how

- Who can use the procedure?
- What exactly does the player do?
- Where (location) does the procedure occur?
- When (turn, time, game state) does it take place?
- How (input) does the player access the procedure?

### Most games have these procedures

- Starting action – how to put the game into play
- Progression of action – ongoing procedures after start
- Special actions – conditional on other elements or game state
- Resolving actions – bring gameplay to a close

## Procedures


### Example – Connect Four

- Choose a player to go first
- On their turn, each player drops one of their colour checkers down any of the slots in the top of the grid
- Play alternates until one of the players gets four checks in their colour in a row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal)

- System procedures
- Work behind the scenes to respond to situations and player actions
- Combat system – calculate how much damage is taken, based on character and weapon attributes
- Board/paper games – this is calculated by the players
- Digital games can have far more complex system procedures

## Rules


### Define game objects and allowable actions by the player

- How do players learn the rules?
- How are the rules enforced?
- What kinds of rules work best in certain situations?
- Are there patterns to rule sets? What can we learn from those patterns?
- Too many rules --\> unplayable, too few rules --\> too simple
- The more complex the rules, the harder to understand

### Rules define objects/concepts, restrict actions, determine effects

- Objects/concepts can be entirely made up or based on real-world (e.g., chess objects are abstractions of real-world, but not poker)
  - Digital game objects can be more complex / numerous
  - How will players learn the nature of objects? Do they evolve?
- Restrictions – can overlap with players or boundaries
- Effects – triggered based on certain circumstances

## Resources


### Resources are assets that can be used to accomplish goals

- Most games use some form of resources

### Designer manages resources and determines how/when to control player access to them

- What resources do you offer players?
- How do you control player access to resources to maintain challenge?

### Resources need utility and scarcity

- Otherwise, they are useless or worthless

## Resources


### Common resource categories

- Lives (action/arcade games – limited number to complete goals)
- Units (strategy, same/different, static/evolve, finite/renewable, cost))
- Health (dramatise loss of life, increase health – pickup/rest)
- Currency (in-game economy, allows trade, role-playing)
- Actions (move/turns - card/board games, special/powerful actions)
- Power-ups (boost player's variables, scarce, limited – time/number)
- Inventory (objectives, purchase/find, power balanced to scarcity)
- Special Terrain (strategy/board games, occupy/control)
- Time (restricts actions/phases, inherently dramatic force)

## Conflict


### Emerges from players trying to accomplish the goals of the game within its rules/boundaries

- Rules/procedures do not allow players to accomplish goals directly
  - Offer inefficient means to accomplish objectives
- Challenge players, forcing them to employ skills
  - Creates a sense of competition/play
  - Enjoyable in some way
  - Leads to a sense of achievement

### Sources of conflict

- Obstacles – physical/mental challenges/puzzles
- Opponents – primary conflict in multiplayer games
- Dilemmas – choices player has to make

## Boundaries


### Separates the game from everything that is not the game

- Agree to play and enter the "magic circle", but the game is temporary and you can leave (feeling of safety)
- Designer defines boundaries and how the player will enter and exit
- Physical (playing field) or conceptual (social agreement to play)
- Emotional – separate game from life (permission to act differently without external consequences)

### Why are boundaries important?

- What if you played football with no boundaries?
- What if you could add money to Monopoly? Or extra cards to poker?

## Outcome


### Measurable and unequal win condition

- Producing a winner is the end state (most games)
  - Simulation, sandpit, open world, ongoing worlds
- Outcome must be uncertain to hold attention

### Types of outcomes relates to interaction patterns and objectives

- Zero-sum games
- Ranking systems
- Statistics
- Multiple objectives

## The Structure of Games


### Formal elements form the structure of a game

- Players - How many? Requirements? Roles?
- Objectives - What is the objective of the game?
- Procedures - What are the required actions for play?
- Rules - Any limits on player actions? Rules for behaviour?
- Resources – What objects are valuable to players? Why?
- Conflict – What causes conflict in the game?
- Boundaries – What are they? Are they physical/conceptual?
- Outcome – What are the potential outcomes?
