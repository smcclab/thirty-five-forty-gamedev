## Brainstorming

- Formalised system of idea generation
- Brainstorming is a skill and takes practice
- Brainstorming with others is stimulating and productive

<aside class="slide-aside">

- Challenges – Design a game:
- "in which players must make strong alliances and then betray them" (experience goal)
- "with a special role for parents to play together with their children" (audience focused)
- "that makes interesting use of only one button for control" (technology driven)

</aside>

- Brainstorming best practices
- State a challenge (set a challenge for the session)
- No criticism (do not self-edit or criticise/ignore others)
- You want free thinking and to build ideas on others
- Try "yes, and" to help naturally build ideas
- Vary the method (different structures suit different people)
- Playful environment (leave your desk, take some toys)
- Put it on the wall (get up, talk/think/sketch/write)
- Go for lots of ideas (try to get 100 ideas)
- Don't go too long (need a break after 60 mins)

## Alternative Methods

- List creation (list everything on a topic, then variations)
- Idea cards (write single ideas/words on index cards)
  - Mix them up, draw 2-4, generate ideas on combinations
- Mind map (start with a core idea, radiate others out)
  - Use lines and different coloured markers to connect ideas
- Stream of consciousness (write anything that comes to mind)
  - Don't self-edit or worry about style, read over after 10 mins
- Shout it out (and record for 5 mins, then listen/transcribe)
- Cut it up (random words/images from print media)
  - Mix and match the pile of pieces, generate ideas
- Surrealist games (more formal games, Exquisite Corpse)
- Research (a subject of interest, rather than randomness)
  - Could get physical experience (e.g., a game about fishing)

<aside class="slide-aside">

- Mind mapping:
- Start with core game concept in centre, then map verbs/actions and associated feelings around the central concept
- Exquisite Corpse:
- Write an article and adjective on paper, fold and pass to neighbour, write a noun - fold/pass, verb – fold/pass, article/adjective - fold/pass, read the poem aloud
- "The exquisite corpse shall drink the new wine"

</aside>

## Brainstorming Tips

- The Write Answer – sit and write down ideas
- Write or Type? - whichever works best for you
- Sketch – draw your ideas
- Toys – bring some toys along to brainstorm
- Change Your Perspective – go to different places
- Immerse Yourself – learn everything you can about your idea
- Crack Jokes – loosen our minds and open new perspectives
- Spare No Expense – use the materials you need to brainstorm
- The Writing on the Wall – whiteboard, cards, post-its, butchers' paper
- The Space Remembers – easier to remember where things are in space
- Write Everything – write down every idea, don't wait for the one
- Number Your Lists – number (not order) ideas on the list
- Destroy your Assumptions – list assumptions then try to break them
- Mix and Match Categories – technology, mechanics, story, aesthetic
- Talk to Yourself – saying things out loud makes them more real
- Find a Partner – find the right partner to bounce ideas off

## Editing and Refining

- Brainstorming generates lots of ideas, but no game
- Need to edit and refine list of ideas
- Edit on a different day than brainstorming

### Reasons/lenses for editing ideas

- Technical feasibility (can you make it?)
  - Might not be feasible yet, or not feasible for your team
- Market opportunity (will people buy it?)
  - Affected by world events, economy, tech cycles, trends
- Artistic considerations (do you love the idea?)
  - Follow your passion
- Business/cost restrictions (do you have the resources?)
  - If you can't scale it down, cut it from the list

## Narrowing the list

- Shortlist 5-10 ideas and thoroughly discuss (and consider merits of each idea, stay positive, compare strengths)
- Narrow list to 3 ideas (and schedule a new brainstorming session to expand these)

<aside class="slide-aside">

- Medal of Honor:
- Razor - "GoldenEye set in WWII on a PlayStation"
- Slogan - "Prepare for your finest hour"
- Informal feedback group:
- Does your idea appeal to players?
- If not, it's easy to change

</aside>

- Second-level brainstorming
- Focus in on features, define the "X" of the game (creative centre) - the "razor" and the "slogan"
- The "razor" cuts – allows the team to determine which features belong and which do not
- The "slogan" is catchy – allows marketing and players to determine if it sounds like something they want to do
- When you have a clear idea of key features and X
- Write 1-2 paragraphs to capture basic gameplay and X
- Hold an informal feedback group (Chapter 9)

## Choosing an Idea

- Something changes when you pick an idea and decide to make it
- Finished design should make it through the 8 filters

<aside class="slide-aside">

- When you choose an idea:
- You think about it differently
- Flaws/benefits are clearer
- You can change your mind

</aside>

- \#15 The Lens of Eight Filters
- Artistic Impulse – does this game feel right?
- Demographics – will the intended audience like it?
- Experience design – is this a well-designed game?
- Innovation – is it novel enough?
- Business and marketing – will it be profitable?
- Engineering – is it technically possible to build it?
- Social/Community - does it meet our social/community goals?
- Playtesting – do the playtesters enjoy the game?

## Turning ideas into a game

- The only way to know if a game works is to play it

### Focus on the Formal Elements

- What are the rules and procedures?
- What actions do the players take and when?
- Are there turns? How do they work?
- How many players can play?
- How long does a game take to resolve?
- What is the working title?
- Who is the target audience?
- What platforms will this game run on?
- What restrictions or opportunities does that environment have?

## Turning ideas into a game

- The only way to know if a game works is to play it

### To expand on the game structure, consider:

- Define the player's goal(s)
- What does a player need to do to win?
- Write down the single most important type of player action in the game. Describe how this functions.
- Write down the procedures and rules in outline format
- Only focus on the most critical rules
- Leave all other rules until later
- Map out how a typical turn or core loop works. Using a flowchart is the most effective way to visualise this.
- Define how many players can play
- How do these players interact with one another?
