## What is play? (cont.)

  - What is play? Playful animals
- Play comes naturally to animals of a certain level of intelligence (e.g., pets)
- Easy to start playing a game based on simple, understandable rules
  - Catch the ball, retrieve the stick, my hand is prey, obstacle course
- Young animals are particularly playful
  - Almost anything in their environment is an excuse to initiate gameplay
  - Without anyone teaching the animal how to engage in this behaviour
- Play is fundamental to development and survival
  - Young animals refine skills that are necessary to hunt, fight, mate, hide and many other activities that are key to survival as an adult

## What is play? Types of play (cont.)

  - What is a game? How are games/play related/different?
- “paidia” and “ludus” for playing and gaming (Caillois, 1961)
  - Two poles of play activities
  - Paidia - uncontrolled, free-form, improvisational
  - Ludus - restricted, structured, goal-focused

- Freeform play (paidia) — Rule-based play (ludus)
- Competitive (agon) — Unregulated athletics (foot racing, wrestling) — Boxing, billiards, fencing, checkers, football, chess
- Chance-based (alea) — Counting-out rhymes (ini mini miny moe) — Betting, roulette, lotteries
- Make-believe (mimicry) — Children's initiations, masks, disguises — Theatre, spectatorship
- Vertigo (ilinx) — Children "whirling", horseback riding, waltzing — Skiing, mountain climbing, tightrope walking

## How are games and play related/different?

- Salen & Zimmerman (2003):
  - Games – constraints of the rules are the rigid structure
  - Play – freedom of players to act within those rules
- Games are rule-bound activities with goals and at least one player who tries to fulfil the goals
- Games are a form of play with structure
- Games provide a safe context in which lessons can be learned through play

## What is a game?

- A game is:
  - A closed, formal system, that
  - Engages players in structured conflict, and
  - Resolves its uncertainty in an unequal outcome.
  - Games:
    - Are entered wilfully
    - Have goals, conflict, rules, challenge
    - Can be won and lost
    - Are interactive
    - Can create their own internal value
    - Engage players
    - Are closed, formal systems

## What does it mean to play a game?

- Games involve accepting imposed boundaries and rules to your behaviour - "suspension of disbelief", "magic circle"
  - Understand/accept that the gameplay experience is an artificial one
  - Understand/accept that games are an abstract construct, governed by a set of formalised rules
  - Understand difference between rules that govern reality and those that govern the make-believe world
  - Also crucial to the enjoyment of film, literature, art, music
- Lusory attitude (Suits, 1978)
  - Psychological attitude required of a player entering into the play of a game
  - To adopt a lusory attitude is to accept the arbitrary rules of a game, to facilitate the resulting experience of play

## What is a game experience?

- Designing a game is like being a party host (Fullerton)
  - You get everything ready (food, drinks, music), then open the doors and see what happens
  - Results are not always predictable or what you envisioned
  - An interactive experience that is only fully realised after your guests arrive
  - The party will vary based on your guests
- The game is not the experience (Schell)
  - The game enables/affords the experience
  - We cannot create experiences, we can only create games
  - The experience exists between the player and the game
