## What are mechanics?

- Procedures and rules of a game
- Describe the goal of the game, how players can/can't try to achieve it, what happens when they try
- Mechanics make a game a game (and not a movie etc)
- Mechanics mainly fall into the categories:
  - Space
  - Time
  - Objects
  - Actions
  - Rules
  - Skill
  - Chance

## Space

- Games take place in a space (magic circle)
- Defines places that can exist and how they are related
- As a mechanic, space is a mathematical construct
  - Consider the abstract construction of a game space
    - Discrete or continuous
    - Number of dimensions
    - Bounding areas, connected or not
- Nested spaces – spaces within spaces
  - e.g., continuous outdoor area, locations that lead to indoor spaces (e.g., RPG, campaign maps)
- Zero dimensions
  - No physical space representation (e.g., 20 questions)
- Consider the space first without the aesthetics
  - What is the abstract construction?

## Time

- Real world – we travel forwards though time with no control
  - Interesting game proposition to play with / control time
- Discrete and continuous time
  - Turns = discrete time steps, no time between turns
  - Sports / most videogames = real-time (continuous)
  - Some games use a mix of time systems
- Clocks and races
  - Clocks used to set absolute time limits for different activities
    - Sand timer in Boggle, sports rounds
    - Can be nested (e.g., basketball)
  - Races – not fixed time limit, but time pressure
    - Car/foot races, Space Invaders
- Controlling time
  - Time-out, pause, speed up, rewind (retry/reload), Braid

## The Lens of Time (lens 27)

- What is it that determines the length of my gameplay activities?
- Are my players frustrated because the game ends too early? How can I change that?
- Are my players bored because the game goes on too long? How can I change that?
- Would clocks or races make my gameplay more exciting?
- Time limits can irritate players. Would I be better off without time limits?
- Would a hierarchy of time structures help my game? That is, several short rounds that together comprise a larger round?

## Objects

- Games spaces have objects in them
  - Characters, props, tokens, scoreboards
- Objects have one or more attributes
  - Categories of information about objects (position, speed)
  - Each attribute has a current state
  - If objects are nouns, attributes are adjectives
  - Attributes can be static or dynamic
  - Some state changes need to be communicated to the player
- Objects that behave the same should look the same, vice-versa
- Can be helpful to construct a state diagram
- Secrets - what information is public or private?
  - Is the game about trying to guess/gather unknown info?
  - Or using complete info to devise strategies?
  - What does the game "know" and how does it use this info?

## Actions

- Actions are the verbs of game mechanics
  - What can players do in the game?
- Two types of actions: basic action, strategic action
  - Basic = base actions a player can take
    - Move a checker forward, jump an opponent
    - Building blocks of gameplay
  - Strategic = use basic actions to achieve a goal
    - Protect a checker, sacrifice a checker
    - Meaningful in the larger picture of the game
- Emergent gameplay
  - Strategic actions not part of the rules, emerge from play
  - Create the playground, nurture the emergent play

<div class="image-credit">Mechanics — Schell p.166-210</div>

## Actions

- Cultivating emergent gameplay
  - Add more verbs (basic actions)
    - More potential for combinations/interactions
    - Too many can lead to bloated/confusing gameplay
  - Verbs that can act on many objects
    - Increase the interconnections / interactions
  - Goals that can be achieved in more than one way
    - Incentivise players to look for interesting strategies
    - Different strategies need to be balanced
  - Many subjects (instances)
    - Having multiple checkers versus having just one
  - Strategic actions scale with (subjects x verbs x objects)
  - Side effects that change constraints
    - Multiple aspects of the game change with each basic action

- How many verbs do my players have?
- How many objects can each verb act on?
- How many ways can players achieve their goals?
- How many subjects do the players control?
- How do side effects change constraints?
- \#31 The Lens of Action
- What are the basic actions in my game?
- What are the strategic actions?
- What strategic actions would I like to see? How can I change my game in order to make those possible?
- Am I happy with the ratio of strategic to basic actions?
- What actions do players wish they could do in my game that they cannot? Can I enable these, as basic or strategic actions?

## Rules

- Define the space, timing, objects, actions, consequences, constraints, and goals
- Parlett's Rule Analysis – different kinds of rules:
  - Operational: what players do to play the game
  - Foundational: underlying formal structure of the game, abstract, mathematical representation of game state and how/when it changes
  - Behavioural: implicit to gameplay, "good sportspersonship"
  - Written: "rules that come with the game"
  - Laws: "tournament rules" for serious, competitive play
  - Official: merge written rules with laws for serious play
  - Advisory: "rules of strategy", tips to play better
  - House: player tune the rules to their own tastes or in response to perceived deficiencies or to mix it up

## Rules

- Modes: different rules during different parts of play
  - Player can get confused with too many modes
  - Often one main mode and then several sub-modes
- Enforcer: who enforces the rules?
  - Traditional games – players enforce the rules
  - Computer games – computer enforces the rules
    - Allows more complex rules, rules become constraints
    - Players still need to be able to comprehend the rules
- Cheatability: perceived/actual cheating, ability to cheat
  - Devalues the game and its endogenous value
- Object of the game: the most important rule, it must be:
  - Concrete: players understand / can clearly state the goal
  - Achievable: players think they have a chance to achieve it
  - Rewarding: challenging, rewards, anticipation of rewards

## The Lens of Goals (lens 32)

- What is the ultimate goal of my game?
- Is that goal clear to players?
- If there a series of goals, do the players understand that?
- Are the different goals related to each other in a meaningful way?
- Are my goals concrete, achievable, and rewarding?
- Do I have a good balance of short- and long-term goals?
- Do players have a chance to decide on their own goals?

- \#33 The Lens of Rules
- What are the foundational rules of my game? How do these differ from the operational rules?
- Are there "laws" or "house rules" that are forming as the game develops? Should these be incorporated into my game directly?
- Are there different modes in my game? Do these modes make things simpler, or more complex? Would the game be better with fewer modes? More modes?
- Who enforces the rules?
- Are the rules easy to understand, or is there confusion about them? If there is confusion, should I fix it by changing the rules or by explaining them more clearly?

## Skill

- Matching skill to challenge is core for enjoyment (Week 3 Theory)
- Most games require a blend of different skills
  - Physical: involve strength, dexterity, coordination, endurance, sports, game controllers, physical controllers
  - Mental: memory, observation, puzzle solving, interesting decision making requires mental skills
  - Social: reading/fooling an opponent, coordinating with teammates, communication, intimidation
- Real versus virtual skills
  - Real skills that the player has
  - Virtual skills that the player is pretending to have
- Enumerating skills – make a list of skills required
  - Could be general or very specific
  - Is your game actually testing the skills that you want it to? (e.g., quick reactions versus memorising patterns)

## The Lens of Skill (lens 34)

- What skills does my game require from the player?
- Are there categories of skill that this game is missing?
- Which skills are dominant?
- Are these skills creating the experience I want?
- Are some players much better at these skills than others? Does this make the game feel unfair?
- Can players improve their skills with practice, leading to a feeling of mastery?
- Does this game demand the right level of skill?

- \#42 The Lens of Head and Hands
- Are my players looking for mindless action or an intellectual challenge?
- Would adding more places that involve puzzle solving in my game make it more interesting?
- Are there places where the player can relax their brain and just play the game without thinking?
- Can I give the player a choice – succeed either by exercising a high level of dexterity or by finding a clever strategy that works with a minimum of physical skill?
- If “1” means all physical and “10” means all mental, what number would my game get?

## Chance

- Chance means uncertainty, which means surprises, fun!
- Humans seek to gain pleasure and avoid pain
  - People will pay a price to eliminate chance of regret / losing something (e.g., insurance)
  - Risk versus reward mechanics
  - Manipulative monetisation mechanics
- Skill and chance – entangled

### \#41 The Lens of Skill vs Chance

- Are my players here to be judged (skill) or to take risks (chance)?
- Skill tends to be more serious than chance: is my game serious or casual?
