# COMP3540 source materials

A survey of `../comp3540-materials/` (the OneDrive copy of the 2023 and 2024
offerings, ~13GB, 184 files, not in this repo) and what it would take to turn
it into this site. Written 2026-09-21, before any content conversion.

The 2023 and 2024 trees are near-duplicates at the top level; **2024 is the
newer and better-organised offering and should be the source of truth**, with
2023 consulted for the things 2024 dropped.

All of this material was written by **Professor Penny Kyburz**
(penny.kyburz@anu.edu.au), who convened the course in 2023 and 2024. Confirm
attribution and reuse with her before publishing any of it.

## Conversion notes

`scripts/extract-ooxml.py` pulls the text out of every `.pptx` and `.docx`
without unpacking the media (it reads only the slide/document XML from the
zip, so a 1.2GB file costs nothing). Point it at the materials tree:

```sh
python3 scripts/extract-ooxml.py ../comp3540-materials /tmp/comp3540-text
```

### The deck converter

`scripts/convert-pptx-decks.py` regenerates every lecture deck. The decks are
**generated**: edit the converter and re-run it, never hand-edit a deck.

The source template is regular enough to model properly rather than scrape.
Each "short copy" slide is two columns: a content placeholder (`p:ph idx="1"`)
whose first paragraph is the heading and whose body paragraphs carry indent
levels, and a sidebar (`p:ph type="body" idx="13"`) holding a topic label,
the reading citations, and on about a third of slides some real explanatory
bullets. Footers, slide numbers and dates are typed placeholders
(`ftr`/`sldNum`/`dt`) and are dropped structurally. What the converter does:

- body paragraphs nest relative to the shallowest level used, and a paragraph
  that drops back to level 0 mid-body becomes an `###` sub-heading;
- a slide whose content does not start at level 0 continues the previous
  slide, so it gets `"<previous heading> (cont.)"` rather than promoting a
  bullet to a heading;
- the sidebar's extra bullets become an `<aside class="slide-aside">`; its
  label and citations become the `.image-credit` line;
- Schell's numbered "lenses" get their own named slide;
- soft line breaks (`<a:br/>`) split text, or headings run together;
- tables become Markdown tables only when PowerPoint marks a header row
  (`firstRow="1"`), otherwise a list — the build's axe check rejects a table
  with invented or empty header cells;
- identical speaker notes repeated across a deck are kept once, in a
  ```comment fence that astromotion does not render;
- every deck's stale "Visit Wattle" closing slide is replaced with a generated
  one.

### Images: nothing is published by default

Two traps here, both of which the converter is built to avoid:

1. **The orphan PNGs are webcam stills of the lecturer.** Each slide's
   relationships still list a 960x540 PNG that no shape references — the
   poster frame of the narration video that was stripped. There are 294 of
   them and they are photographs of a person's face. The converter only ever
   reads image blips reached from a `<p:pic>` element, never from the
   relationship list, so these are never touched. Do not "simplify" that.
2. **Most of what is on the slides is other people's copyright.** Of 436
   `<p:pic>` shapes, 374 are Microsoft stock icons (named `Graphic N`, exactly
   1x1 inch) and only 62 are real pictures. Those 62 — 49 distinct — were
   reviewed on 2026-09-21: commercial game screenshots, scanned figures from
   the set texts (some still showing Fullerton's figure numbers), and stock
   photography. Fine in a PowerPoint behind Wattle under the educational
   statutory licence; not fine on a public website.

So the converter publishes an image only if its SHA-256 appears in
`scripts/deck-images.json`, which is **empty**. Every omission is recorded in
the deck source as a ```comment naming the citation it sat beside, so a future
convenor can see what was there. `scripts/deck-images.README.md` explains how
to review and allow-list. To re-review:

```sh
python3 scripts/convert-pptx-decks.py --review /tmp/deck-images
```

Things the extracted text needs cleaned up:

- **Footer boilerplate on every slide.** `ANU SCHOOL OF COMPUTING   |   GAME
  DEVELOPMENT - WEEK N …`, the slide number, and a date like `22 JUL 24` land
  in the middle of each slide's text because they are shapes on the master.
  Strip them.
- **Reading citations** (`Fullerton p.102-103`) are separate shapes too, and
  are worth keeping — they become a citation line on the slide.
- **Speaker notes carry real content**, especially in the workshop decks
  (exercise references, background for the tutor). Decide per deck whether
  they belong on the page or in a `comment` fence.
- **Slide order is not reading order.** These are visually laid-out slides;
  the text comes out in shape order, which is roughly but not reliably
  top-to-bottom.
- **The media is the lecture.** A theory deck with 69 images and 12 videos
  does not survive as text. Any real conversion has to extract
  `ppt/media/*` and re-reference it, and decide what to do about the video
  (most of it is third-party clips that cannot be republished).
- **Poll Everywhere slides** are live-poll placeholders and should be dropped
  or replaced with a static prompt.
- **Student data must not be published**: the peer assessment `.xlsx` files,
  `GameDev-Tutors.docx`, and the `WW4-Photos` (people are identifiable
  without consent to publish).

## Suggested approach

1. **Assessments first.** The five `.docx` specs are self-contained, convert
   almost losslessly to Markdown, and are what students most need. This is
   the highest value for the least work.
2. **Workshops next.** The workshop decks are small, structured
   ("Before the workshop" / "In the workshop" / activities with timings) and
   map directly onto a content page per week — which suits the web better
   than a slide deck does.
3. **Theory last, and probably not as decks.** Either write new decks in
   `src/decks/` using the 2024 topic split as the outline, or publish the
   theory as content pages with the images extracted and the videos linked
   rather than embedded. A mechanical pptx→deck conversion would produce 18
   decks of stripped bullet points with the images missing, which is worse
   than either.
